Case: '482 patent — {{party_b}} (licensee/defendant) vs {{party_a}} (patent holder)

SETTING (2010)

It is 2010. {{party_b}} is preparing to release a new smartphone with a built-in camera and multimedia messaging service (MMS) functionality. MMS allows users to send photos via text messages.

The parties agree that the '482 patent is valid and infringed by {{party_b}} smartphones that include the MMS photo-upload/sharing feature.

The following information has been made available to both parties.

{{party_b}} BUSINESS CONTEXT

- Average selling price per phone: $228.23
- Profit margin: 19.11% (approx. $43.61 in profit per phone)
- Projected smartphone sales during the patent term: 103.6 million units

CAMERA / MMS CONTRIBUTION

- {{party_b}} internal data: camera accounts for 6.2% of phone production cost (approx. $14.15).
- Industry surveys:
  - 65% of users use the phone's camera for photos (not video)
  - Of those, 77% share their photos
  - Of those, 41% use MMS rather than email or web
- Together: 20.5% of users use the infringing MMS feature.

COMPARABLE LICENSES

- Facebook paid $20 million for a non-exclusive, fully paid-up license to the '482 patent.
- BlackBerry paid $3 million for a similar license (2010: still a strong communications player, position eroding).
- eBay paid $3 million for a full image processing suite.
- iPIX paid $735,000 for a license (smaller image-processing / virtual-tour company).

INDUSTRY CONTEXT

- A 2012 industry estimate: over 250,000 active patents cover smartphone technologies.
- Users select smartphones based on many factors (screen size, apps, hardware), not just MMS.

IF NEGOTIATIONS FAIL

- Dispute proceeds to federal court litigation; the '482 patent is expected to be found valid and infringed. Uncertainty is remedies only.
- Injunction likelihood: {{party_a}} is a small technology company that competes directly with {{party_b}} in the mobile device market. Under eBay Inc. v. MercExchange, probability of an injunction is high (historically around 80%).
- Damages exposure: {{party_b}} would face a reasonable-royalty award based on what a jury believes would have occurred in a successful hypothetical negotiation.

PRIOR {{party_a}} DEMAND (EARLIER LETTER)

- Per-unit royalty of approx. $0.28/phone → ~$29 million over the life of the patent.
- Calculation: profit per phone ($43.61) × camera cost share (6.2%) × infringing-feature usage (20.5%) × 50/50 split.

CONFIDENTIAL GOALS — {{party_b}}

- Likelihood of an injunction is high (roughly 80%); an injunction is catastrophic — avoid trial.
- Attorney's fees are estimated at $5 million, excluding appeals.
- Secure a lump-sum license quickly to prevent delays in smartphone launch.
- Optimal settlement range: $500,000 to $2 million, with flexibility depending on risk of delay.
- Compare {{party_b}}'s position to companies that paid smaller lump-sum royalties (e.g. BlackBerry $3M, eBay $3M, iPIX $735K — not Facebook's $20M).
- Highlight broader smartphone features and the weak connection between sending photos over text and the overall value of the smartphone.
